# Use what you know to decide what to do

## Theories as action generators

### David R. MacIver

**Action Generator**

Something that prompts you to act in the world

They can have one of two results:

- Do *nothing*
- Do *something*:
   - Set of actions it *can* generate
   - Subset of actions it *encourages* you to take

Theories act as action generators

> It’s worth having a stable of bad theories lying around that
> generate actions that you wouldn’t otherwise have thought of
> and that are basically harmless in the worst case, and possibly
> interesting in the best case.

## Acquiring and using knowledge

1. Aquire as *little* information as possible
   1. Forces you to learn the *right* information
   1. Forces you to do thinkgs you can actually achieve soon
   1. Prevents you from being distracted my the immensity of the task
1. Acquire information as efficiently as possible
   1. (Spell) Look at spellbook for `Knowledge Acquisition`

### Being effective early when joining a company

1. Use the information you acquire as effectively as possible
   1. Value of Knowledge is effectively:
      1. If I do X, what is the outcome (X -> R) ?
         1. Solve by Mental Models (f:X->R)
            - Implicit f: Black box; You know the answer without
               and explicit description of the function (intuition)
            - Explicit f: Think through all steps to reach the outcome. Typically
               more work and more accurate, and articulate-able
      1. If I want Y, what should I do?
         1. Answering this, is looking at
            the universe of {f:X->R}->Y. Everything else
            is a optimisation for that search function
            - Generate ideas (using f:X->Rs and first-principles) of X->Rs
               and sort by what could work
            - Put proper though into your *candidates* and decide
               which is the best
